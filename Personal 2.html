1.Jollof and Chicken

Ghanaian jollof rice is a one-pot dish made by cooking rice directly in a rich, spiced tomato and onion stew

Ingredients

  1. Tomatoes: Fresh plum/Roma tomatoes or tomato puree/passata
  2. Tomato Paste: Essential for deep color and rich flavor (brands like Onga or standard canned paste)
  3. Onions,Peppers,Aromatics,Oil,Stock Cubes,Salt
  4. Rice,Chicken

How We Prepare It

  • Blend the base: Roughly chop 2 medium onions, 3 large plum tomatoes, 1-2 scotch bonnet peppers (adjust for heat), a thumb-sized piece of ginger, and 4 garlic cloves.
  • Process: Blend until completely smooth. Set aside.
  • Heat the oil: Pour 1/2 cup of vegetable oil into a large, deep pot over medium heat.
  • Sauté onions: Add 1 finely sliced onion and fry for about 3–5 minutes until translucent and slightly golden.
  • Add tomato paste: Stir in 4 tablespoons of tomato paste. Fry this mixture continuously for 5–7 minutes. The paste should darken slightly and separate from the oil. Do not skip this step; it removes the bitter, metallic taste of the paste.
  • Pour in the blend: Add your blended tomato and pepper mixture to the fried paste and stir well.
  • Season the stew: Add 2 bay leaves, 1 teaspoon of curry powder, 1 teaspoon of dried thyme, 1/2 teaspoon of nutmeg, and 2 crushed stock cubes (like Maggi or Onga). Add salt to taste.
  • Simmer: Turn the heat down to medium-low. Cover the pot and let the stew simmer for 15–20 minutes. Stir occasionally. It is ready when the oil rises to the top and the stew thickens.Reserve some stew (Optional): Scoop out about 1 cup of the cooked stew and set it aside. You can add it back later if the rice looks too dry.
  • Wash the rice: Thoroughly rinse 3 cups of Jasmine or Basmati rice under cold water until the water runs clear. Drain completely. Washing removes excess starch so your jollof doesn't become soggy or sticky.Combine: Pour the drained rice into the pot with the remaining stew. Stir well for 2–3 minutes to ensure every grain of rice is coated in the hot oil and stew.Add liquid: Pour in 2 cups of meat stock (chicken or beef) or water. The liquid should just barely cover the surface of the rice. Stir once and taste the liquid—it should taste slightly oversalted, as the rice will absorb the salt.
  • Trap the steam: Cover the pot tightly with a layer of aluminum foil or a clean plastic bag, then put the pot lid tightly on top. This traps the steam, which cooks the rice evenly without adding too much water.Low heat: Turn the heat down to the lowest setting.Turn the rice: Let it cook undisturbed for 15 minutes. Open the pot and gently scoop the rice from the bottom to the top using a large spoon or a carving fork. Do not stir vigorously, or the grains will break.Final steam: Cover it tightly again and let it steam for another 15–20 minutes until the rice is completely tender and the grains separate easily.

2. Ampesie and Kontomire

Ampesi with kontomire stew is a classic Ghanaian comfort dish made from plain boiled root vegetables or plantains served with a rich, savory cocoyam leaf stew

Ingredients

  1. For the Ampesi: 1 medium tube of Yam or 4 unripe/half-ripe Plantains
  2. For the Kontomire: 2 big bunches of fresh Kontomire leaves (can substitute with baby spinach).
  3. Aromatics & Heat: 1 medium onion, 2-3 tomatoes, and scotch bonnet peppers (to taste).
  4. The Traditional Secret: A small piece of Momoni (fermented fish) or Koobi (salted tilapia).
  5. Oil: 1/4 cup of authentic Palm Oil.Protein & Sides (Essential Garnishes): Boiled eggs, 1 canned mackerel (optional), and a sliced avocado.

How We Prepare It

  • Peel the starches: Cut your yam into rounds and peel off the skin. If using plantains, slice off the ends, make a shallow slit down the length, and peel them off.
  • Boil: Place the yam or plantains into a pot, add enough water to cover them, and add 1 teaspoon of salt.
  • Cook: Bring to a boil over medium-high heat for about 20–30 minutes until a fork slides easily through them. Drain and cover to keep hot.
  • Wash the greens: Wash your kontomire leaves thoroughly with salted water to clear any grit, and trim off the stalks.Soften: Drop the leaves, whole tomatoes, and scotch bonnet peppers into a small pot with a little water. Boil gently for 8–10 minutes until the leaves soften and lose their raw texture. Drain well.
  • Grind aromatics: First, slice half of your raw onion into the Asanka along with the boiled peppers and a pinch of salt. Grind until smooth.Add tomatoes: Peel the skin off the boiled tomatoes, add them to the bowl, and mash them into the mix.Incorporate the greens: Add the boiled kontomire leaves and grind everything together until well combined but still slightly textured.Add the fish: Gently flake in your canned mackerel (leaving out the tomato sauce from the can) or pieces of cooked smoked fish.
  • Fry the momoni: Heat the palm oil in a small pan over medium heat. Drop in your piece of momoni or salted fish and let it fry for 2 minutes until it dissolves and fragrant fumes fill the kitchen.Sauté the remaining onions: Add the remaining half of your onion (sliced) into the hot palm oil. Fry for 3 minutes until translucent and slightly brown.The sizzle: Carefully pour the hot, infused palm oil and onions directly over the ground kontomire mixture in your Asanka. Give it a gentle mix.

3.Fufu and Chicken Soup

Fufu and chicken soup is a classic, comforting Ghanaian meal featuring smooth, starchy fufu paired with a spicy, watery tomato and pepper broth known as light soup.

Ingredients

  1. Chicken: 1 whole Hard chicken (local/layer chicken is highly preferred as it holds up to long boiling and yields the best flavor) cut into medium pieces
  2. Aromatics for steaming: 1 medium onion (sliced), 2 cloves of garlic, a thumb-sized piece of ginger, and 1 aniseed (osieva) or rosemary (all blended together)
  3. Seasoning: 1-2 chicken stock cubes (Maggi or Onga) and salt to taste
  4. Tomatoes,Onions,Peppers,Garden Eggs,Water,Tomato Paste
  5. Cassava,Plantain or Cocoyam

How We Prepare It

  • Place your cleaned chicken pieces into a large, deep soup pot.
  • Pour the blended steaming aromatics (ginger, garlic, 1 onion, and aniseed/rosemary) over the chicken.
  • Add 1-2 stock cubes and 1 teaspoon of salt.
  • Stir well, cover the pot, and let the chicken steam in its own juices over medium-high heat for about 10–12 minutes.
  • Place your whole tomatoes (3-4), whole onions (2), whole peppers, and prepped garden eggs directly into the pot on top of the steaming chicken.Pour in about 3 cups of water (enough to submerge the vegetables).Cover and bring to a boil. Leave it to cook for about 15 minutes until the tomatoes and onions are completely soft and their skins start to crack.
  • Remove and blend: Carefully fish out the boiled tomatoes, onions, and peppers from the soup. Leave the garden eggs and chicken in the pot.Process: Put the boiled vegetables into a blender (or use an Asanka) and blend until completely smooth.Sieve back in: Pour the blended vegetable mixture back into the soup pot through a fine-mesh sieve to catch any tomato skins and seeds. This keeps the soup perfectly light and smooth.Simmer: Add another 3–4 cups of water to thin the soup to a light consistency. Bring the soup back to a boil, then lower the heat to a simmer. Let it cook for another 25–30 minutes until the hard chicken is completely tender and a thin layer of natural red oil forms on the surface. Taste and add salt if needed.
  • Peel the cassava tubers and cut them into medium chunks, removing the woody core string from the center.Peel the unripe plantains and cut them into halves.Place them together in a large pot, cover with water, add a pinch of salt, and boil for 20–25 minutes until a fork easily passes through both. Drain the water but save a small cup of the hot water for pounding.
  • Pound the plantain first: Place the hot plantains into the mortar. Pound them continuously until they form a smooth, yellow, lump-free paste. Remove the plantain paste and set it aside in a bowl.Pound the cassava: Place the hot cassava chunks into the mortar. Pound vigorously until it becomes sticky, white, and smooth.Combine: Add the pounded plantain back into the mortar with the cassava.Turn and drive: Pound them together while the turner continuously dips their hand in water to turn, flip, and fold the mixture. This keeps it from sticking to the mortar and incorporates air.The finish: Continue pounding until the two starches merge completely into a single, stretchy, glossy, and perfectly smooth mound.

4.Kenkey

Kenkey and fish is a popular Ghanaian dish made from fermented cornmeal dough served with fried or grilled fish and spicy pepper sauce

Ingredients

  1. Corn Dough
  2. Wrapping
  3. Water & Salt
  4. Fish,Seasoning,Salt,Oil
  5. Peppers,Onions,Tomatoes,Black Shito

How We Prepare It

  • Divide the dough: Take your fermented corn dough and divide it into two equal halves.
  • Cook the Aflata: Place one half into a pot, add 2 cups of water and a teaspoon of salt, and mix until it forms a smooth, watery paste. Put it on medium heat and stir continuously with a sturdy wooden spatula. It will quickly thicken into a heavy, sticky dough. Cook for about 10 minutes, then remove it from the heat.
  • Mix it back: Immediately add the remaining raw corn dough into the hot, cooked dough. Mix and knead them together vigorously until completely uniform. This mix of cooked and raw dough is what gives kenkey its signature sour, elastic texture.
  • Mould and wrap: Divide the dough into ball-sized portions. Wrap each ball tightly in washed corn husks, tucking the loose ends underneath to seal it. (If using aluminum foil, wrap the ball tightly in cling film first, then double-wrap it in foil).Boil/Steam: Place a few extra corn husks or a metal steamer rack at the bottom of a large pot to prevent burning. Arrange the wrapped kenkey balls tightly inside. Pour in enough water to halfway submerge the balls. Cover tightly and boil over medium-high heat for 45 to 60 minutes until the husks turn yellow-brown and the dough inside is firm.
  • Score the fish: Make 2–3 shallow diagonal cuts on both sides of the cleaned fish so the seasoning penetrates.
  • Season: Rub your blended ginger, garlic, onion, and pepper mix all over the fish, including inside the belly and the cuts. Sprinkle generously with salt. Let it marinate for at least 20 minutes.
  • Fry: Heat vegetable oil in a deep frying pan over medium-high heat. Carefully lower the fish into the hot oil. Fry for 7–10 minutes per side until the skin is beautifully golden-brown and extra crispy. Drain on paper towels.
